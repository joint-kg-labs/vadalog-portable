# ChaseBench — `Ontology-256` scenario, in Vadalog

A runnable transcription of the `Ontology-256` scenario of
[ChaseBench](https://github.com/dbunibas/chasebench) (`scenarios/Ontology-256`) into
the dialect accepted by `vadalog-portable`. The programs are versioned with the
repository; **the data is not** — it is 482 MB — so `fetch-data.sh` brings it down
from ChaseBench on demand. Nothing here ships in a wheel or a release, and whatever a
run writes under `out*/` is ignored by git. Provenance and citation:
[`../README.md`](../README.md).

`Ontology-256` is the largest scenario of the benchmark and the largest thing this
engine has been asked to do: **218 source relations of about 10 000 rows each, 256
source-to-target TGDs, 273 target TGDs and 348 target EGDs**, the latter expanding to
921 Vadalog rules. Like [`STB-128`](../STB-128/) it is generated by
[iBench](https://github.com/RJMillerLab/ibench) from schema-mapping primitives — the
relation names say which: `_ad_` add attribute, `_de_` delete attribute, and the
vertical-partition family `_vi_`, `_vh_`, `_vnm_` — but at twice the size and with a
target schema deep enough that a rule's head often feeds another rule's body.

## Files

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `rules.vada` | the 256 s-t TGDs, the 273 target TGDs and the EGDs |
| `inputs-10k.vada` | `@input` / `@bind` for the 218 source relations |
| `Ontology-256-10k.vada` | driver: inputs + rules + `@output` of all 444 derived relations |
| `Ontology-256-10k-queries.vada` | the same plus the twenty benchmark queries |
| `fetch-data.sh` | downloads and unpacks the source data into `data/10k` |

Each common-format EGD statement carries several equalities at once; a Vadalog EGD
takes one equality per rule, so the 348 statements become 921 rules sharing their
bodies. Query bodies are reordered so the joins bind early, as in the other scenarios.

## Running

```sh
sh fetch-data.sh                       # once: ~230 MB down, 482 MB unpacked
python3 ../../dist/vadalog.pyz run Ontology-256-10k.vada \
        --format csv --out out-10k --max-facts 30000000
```

**`--max-facts` is not optional here.** The default budget is five million facts
(SPECIFICATION.md §7.7) and this scenario needs seven, so without the flag the run
stops after about two and a half minutes with `the instance exceeded 5000000 facts;
raise --max-facts if the program is expected to be this large` — the guard doing its
job, but on a program that really is this large. The run also wants about 6 GB of
memory, and writes some 640 MB of CSV.

## Results obtained

2 146 490 source facts in; the chase closes in **4 rounds and 6 m 55 s** on CPython
3.10 with an instance of 7 184 521 facts, of which 5 038 031 are the 444 derived
relations; 3 625 198 candidate facts were suppressed by the isomorphism check. Chase
and the twenty queries together: 6 m 40 s.

## What was and was not checked

An independent check, written without the engine, read the engine's own output and
model-checked it. **All 348 target EGDs hold** — the key constraints, the part most
likely to go wrong with existentials in play, come out right — and all twenty answer
counts above were reproduced exactly, `q4` and `q5` included, whose answers are partly
made of marked nulls (406 of 757 and 43 of 73).

What does not hold is the vertical-partition family. **59 of the 273 target TGDs** are
violated, and **47 of the 192 source-to-target TGDs that have a multi-atom head**
(checked with their source relations loaded, 6.3 million facts in the checker at
once). They are all the same shape: a rule whose two head atoms are tied together by
an invented join attribute. When two rows agree on the attribute that goes into one
half, the two halves are isomorphic, the termination check of SPECIFICATION.md §7.4
keeps one, and the surviving partner of the discarded one is left without its match.
[`STB-128`](../STB-128/) shows the same thing one size down, 12 rules out of 160.

The remaining 64 single-atom source-to-target TGDs were left unchecked: they would
have required the rest of the 2.1 million source facts in memory alongside the
instance, which does not fit on the machine this ran on.
